Two days. One working prototype.

  • Day 2 Build the Trimble-looking version.
  • Day 2 Share a live link people can use.
3 / 10

Start with a page people can see

  • Bring a real screen or a real user problem.
  • Describe who it is for and what they need to do.
  • Watch the first result in Preview.
  • Ask for one clear improvement at a time.
4 / 10

Then make it feel like a product

  • A screenshot shows one moment. A product changes.
  • Add one useful interaction: open, select, filter, or confirm.
  • Show what happens before, during, if it fails, and after it works.
  • Let someone else try it without extra explanation.
5 / 10

Bring Figma, Cursor, and Modus together

  • Paste a Figma selection into Cursor.
  • Give Cursor the Modus standards and component guidance.
  • Keep the conversation in Agent and Preview.
  • Compare the result with the design and adjust.
6 / 10

Build the Modus version

  • Use existing Modus components, patterns, and templates.
  • Keep the look, spacing, and language consistent.
  • Check the flow on a phone-width and a wide screen.
  • Make sure people can complete the task with a keyboard.
7 / 10

Practice, feedback, and questions

  • Most of the time is spent building, not watching slides.
  • You will try the work, get feedback, and improve it.
  • Bring questions as they come up.
  • The goal is a prototype you can explain and share.
8 / 10

Share the working result

  • Save the prototype in your GitHub account.
  • Publish a live link another person can open.
  • Share the Figma, the repository, and the preview together.
  • Say clearly what is finished and what still needs work.
9 / 10

You leave able to do this

  • Turn a design idea into a running page.
  • Direct Cursor until the experience feels right.
  • Build with Modus instead of a one-off visual.
  • Share a prototype Product and Engineering can try.
